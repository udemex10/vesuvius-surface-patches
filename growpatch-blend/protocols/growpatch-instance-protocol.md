# Direct sheet identity in the historical Scroll 1 acquisition

An independent cohort uses the separately released historical Scroll 1 CT and harmonized per-sheet instance annotations, in 7.91 µm voxels. This probes physical sheet identity more directly than distance to a curated machine-proposed mesh. The common input for both methods is a thin ridge volume generated from the separately published U-Net foreground. The methods receive the same central seed and orientation. The foreground predictions and the manual instance edits have related provenance. This evaluates tracing given that foreground; it does not validate an upstream surface detector on unseen scans.

Select four development, eight test and four confirmation source cubes before reviewing algorithm outputs. Use the oriented stock, the original recovery and seed-phase recovery at their operating points locked on the 9.6 µm experiment. Apply the same stock-offset control. Do not recalibrate the primary transfer results on the historical labels.

Within the same seed-centered disc and on rays where the designated reference surface is defined, query the integer instance volume at the candidate XYZ. A voxel with the seed's label counts as correct, another nonzero label counts as a known wrong sheet, and zero or an out-of-volume point counts as unassigned. A ray with multiple distinct candidate intersections counts as ambiguous. Provide these areas separately and give the largest seed-connected area on the correct sheet. The main paired check asks for larger seed-connected correct area with both (a) no higher fraction on a known different sheet among classified hits and (b) no greater fraction of accepted area that is wrong, ambiguous or unassigned. This prevents an algorithm gaining apparent safety by moving its errors into empty space. Incomplete annotation can make the second test conservative. Report risk on the original, spatially separated splits and site-by-site dependence. Reference-center distance is a secondary geometry measurement.
